# Silverleaf core segments and subsegments

*As of 2026-10-11, counted from the three databases: the company master, the welfare run `arusha-welfare-2026-09` and the government run `arusha-government-2026-09`. Nothing is sent from the repository; a person sends.*

We write to four kinds of audience. Each has its own reason to talk to us, its own request and its own decision-maker. On top of the four sits one shared layer: how confident we are that a message reaches the person who can say yes.

| Core segment | What they are to us | What we ask | Organisations |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Employers | Customers through their staff | A partnership on an education benefit for staff's children (or sponsorship, for the few that already fund education) | 716 companies + 117 savings groups |
| 2. Introducers | Multipliers | Share the staff benefit with their member employers | 16 |
| 3. Welfare organisations | Partners and funders | A partnership on the education of the children in their care, or sponsorship of students | 444 in scope |
| 4. Government offices | A trusted forum for parents | A free 15–20 minute talk to parents at a community meeting | 150 |

## 1. Employers

Companies whose staff are the parents we want to reach. Most are in tourism: 81% are safari operators, travel agencies, hotels or lodges. The first message asks for a short meeting and states no offer terms. The documented offer follows in the second message.

**Subsegments by message variant**

- **A – Sponsorship (14):** companies with sourced education or community giving. We ask them to sponsor students, at any size.
- **B – Staff-benefit partnership (700):** every other company. We ask for a partnership on an education benefit for staff's children.

**Who receives it:** the most senior person running the Tanzania or Arusha operation: the owner, founder, MD, GM or HR head. A group or global executive is kept as the escalation contact.

## 2. Introducers

Introducers aren't customers. They are associations, chambers of commerce and HR bodies whose members are the employers we want to reach. Their draft asks them to share Silverleaf's staff education benefit with their members, through a newsletter, a members' meeting slot or an introduction. A single yes can reach dozens of companies at once.

| Kind | Organisations |
|---|---|
| Chambers of commerce | TNCC national, TNCC Arusha regional, TNCC Kilimanjaro regional |
| Employers' associations | ATE (national and Northern Zone, Arusha), CTI (national and Arusha/Moshi office), TPSF |
| Sector associations | TATO (tour operators), HAT (hotels), TBA (bankers), TAHA (horticulture), APHFTA (private health facilities), CSSC (Christian social services) |
| HR body | Women HR Association of Tanzania (WHAT) |
| Other | East African Business Council (EABC) |

**Who receives it:** the executive secretary, CEO or secretariat. An elected chair is used when the body names no executive.

## 3. Welfare organisations

Children's homes, programmes, specialised centres and their funders, within the catchment around the five campuses. Two requests:

- **Partnership:** to homes, programmes and centres, on the education of the children in their care, with a short meeting. No offer terms; the partner rate follows in the second message.
- **Sponsorship:** to funders, only with a sourced, verified link to a home or programme near the campuses. Without that link the draft is held.

58 other register entries are recorded as out of scope and are not written to.

**Who receives it:** the leader on the ground, such as the head of the home, the country manager or the Arusha programme director. A senior executive abroad is the escalation contact. A chair is used only when no executive is named, which is common for German, Dutch and Swiss support associations.

## 4. Government offices

Local offices that can convene the community meetings where parents already gather. We ask them for nothing for themselves. We ask for a free talk in Kiswahili on getting children ready for school, with a take-home booklet. Letters are formal Kiswahili on letterhead, addressed to the office by title, and delivered by hand.

| Subsegment | Offices | Order and request |
|---|---|---|
| Councils, pilot (Arusha City, Arusha District, Hai, Meru) | 4 | First: a protocol introduction, asking the council to introduce us to its ward executive officers |
| Councils, other | 5 | After the pilot |
| District and regional offices | 7 districts, 3 regions | Courtesy notice only |
| Wards and villages | 129 wards, 2 villages | Only after the council's introduction; nearest first (55 within 10 km, 33 at 10–20 km, 31 beyond 20 km, 12 not yet located) |

**Who decides:** the council director (DED) for councils, the District or Regional Commissioner, and the ward or village executive officer. Education, community development and social welfare officers are recorded as quick-decision contacts. Names go on the delivery list, not in the letter.

## The confidence layer (all four segments)

Each first message sits in one confidence segment. The Resend import files and the delivery lists follow it.

| Segment | Rule |
|---|---|
| **High** | Ready for review, nothing held, addressed to a named decision-maker the organisation publishes, at an address on its own domain or the person's own published address |
| ↳ **High – direct** | The person's own address, or a role inbox matching their role (hr@, md@, dg@) |
| ↳ **High – indirect** | A general inbox (info@, reservations@) addressed to the named person; each row says what would make it direct |
| **Medium** | Ready, but it goes to the organisation's inbox and needs routing, or to a free-mail inbox the organisation publishes as official |
| **Low** | Held or needs research: red flags, possible closure, an unconfirmed role or inbox, a funder without a verified link, a savings group's or public body's checks |
| **No email** | A phone only, or no route: these go on the call sheets |

| Track | High (direct + indirect) | Medium | Low | No email |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Employers | 232 (29 + 203) | 323 | 48 | 229 |
| Introducers | 12 (2 + 10) | 3 | 0 | 1 |
| Welfare | 67 (12 + 55) | 9 | 118 | 233 |
| Government | 4 (the pilot councils) | 0 | 146 (held until the council introduction) | delivered by hand; 11 offices have an official inbox |

An address two rows share is exported once (19 this week).

**The recipient rule, everywhere:**
- The first message goes to the in-country person who can decide.
- A more senior executive is recorded as the escalation contact. They are never copied on the first message and get no draft of their own.
- They are used if the local office stays silent after the follow-up, declines, or defers to head office.
- Vice chairs, treasurers and secretaries are never chosen.

Rules and counts come from `scripts/messaging/export_resend_contacts.py`, `scripts/messaging/build_company_audience_profile.py`, `scripts/master/rank_employer_reach.py` and the run databases.
