## 2. Business Model — Three Distinct Lines

Silverleaf is not a single-business organisation. It runs three materially different businesses with different customers, sales cycles and buying committees. Any automation design has to account for all three.

### 2.1 Silverleaf Schools Network (B2C — the revenue engine)
Fee-paying parents at five campuses. This is the cash-generating core and the origin of essentially all current revenue.

### 2.2 Silverleaf Teacher Training Institute / Talent Academy (B2B2C)
Pre-service and in-service teacher training, sold to individual trainee teachers and to institutions. Positioned as "Tanzania's premium teacher training institution." Explicitly intended to serve teachers **beyond** the Silverleaf network.

### 2.3 Government & Institutional Partnerships (B2G)
Ministry-level sale of digitised curriculum and AI teacher-coaching tooling. Long cycle, relationship-led, evaluation-gated.

## 13. Market and Competitive Position

### Market size
- Primary pupils in Tanzania: **11,391,185** (2024, World Bank).
- Private share of primary enrolment: **~4.5%** (2021, World Bank) — an addressable base of roughly 500,000 pupils.
- Population ~60–62m, among the world's fastest-growing; ~45–50% under 15.

### Structural drivers
- The **2016 Fee-Free Basic Education Policy** removed public fees, surging enrolment and creating severe capacity and quality pressure.
- Out-of-school children rose from ~800,000 (2010) to **1.8 million (2018)** in absolute terms; 57% of pre-primary-aged children out of school.
- **Over 10,000 schools have pupil-teacher ratios above 100**, some reaching 300, against a national average of ~40. Under half of teachers master their subject.
- RISE/World Bank research finds private schools have excess capacity and show no worse delivery or performance than public counterparts — supportive of PPP models.

### Regulatory environment
- **TIE** develops curriculum and approves learning materials including digital content. **NECTA** runs national examinations. Silverleaf uses the national curriculum, not Cambridge.
- The Competency-Based Curriculum transition is ongoing; academic literature flags significant implementation difficulty.
- National primary instruction is Kiswahili-medium with English from secondary — Silverleaf's English-medium primary offer *is* its core parent-facing value proposition.
- Sector governed jointly by **MoEST** and **PO-RALG**; current **ESDP 2025/26–2029/30** published February 2025.
- **NACTVET** governs teacher training accreditation.

### Competitive landscape

**Structural finding: there is no directly comparable low-fee private school chain in Tanzania.** Bridge International Academies — the archetypal low-cost African chain — does not operate here. The competitive field is thousands of independent, single-site, owner-operated low-fee English-medium schools. That is a consolidation opportunity, but it also means no chain-level benchmark exists.
